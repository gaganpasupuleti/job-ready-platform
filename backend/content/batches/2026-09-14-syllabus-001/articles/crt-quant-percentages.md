# Percentages for placement aptitude

Percent means “per hundred”. A 25% discount on a listed price of 240 means you remove one quarter of 240, not that the sale price becomes 25.

## Learning objectives

- Convert a percentage into a fraction of a whole.
- Compute a single discount or increase without inventing a second step.
- Spot the common trap of treating the discount amount as the final price.

## Worked example

A book costs 240. A 25% discount is applied once.

1. Find 25% of 240: \(240 \times 0.25 = 60\).
2. Subtract once: \(240 - 60 = 180\).

The sale price is 180. The value 60 is the discount, not the price you pay.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Percent of price | 240 × 0.25 | 60 |
| Sale price | 240 − 60 | 180 |

## Common mistakes

- Reporting the discount (60) instead of the sale price (180).
- Applying the discount twice when the problem states it once.
- Treating “25% of 240” as “240 minus 25”.

## Recap

Convert the percent to a decimal or fraction, multiply by the whole once, then add or subtract as the sentence asks. Mark-as-read on this lesson tracks reading only; it is not a placement score.
